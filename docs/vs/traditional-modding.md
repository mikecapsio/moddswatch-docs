# MODDS.WATCH vs Traditional Modding

Traditional modding is how a custom watch actually gets made: parts, tools, and hands. MODDS.WATCH is how you decide the look before that work starts. The site's longer essay on this split is https://modds.watch/blog/digital-vs-traditional-modding

## Short answer

Use MODDS.WATCH when the idea is still a guess, the watch is a client's, or the parts are expensive enough that a wrong combination hurts. Use the bench when you are ready to fit, assemble, and wear the watch. Most good projects use both, in that order.

## What each one does

### MODDS.WATCH

- Free 3D preview on a supported Casio
- Many color and artwork tries in one session
- Pictures a client can approve
- No wear on the real watch while you are still deciding
- Stops at the picture. It does not open the case

### Traditional modding

- Real parts, real fit, real finishing
- The skills that make the watch wearable
- The only way to know a gasket, a hand stack, or a crystal actually works
- Slow and costly if you use the bench as the sketchbook

## When the lab earns its place

- You are comparing more than one direction
- Someone else has to agree before you spend their money
- The face is custom art and you need to see it at watch scale
- You are new and you want a plan before you buy a kit you will not use

## When to skip the lab and work on the watch

- You have done this exact swap many times and nothing aesthetic is undecided
- You are practicing hand setting, case opening, or regulation. Software will not teach your hands
- The watch is not a live model

## A hybrid that matches how the work really goes

1. Explore in the lab. Keep two or three exported options.
2. Get a yes on one picture.
3. Source parts that can produce that look. Confirm compatibility with the seller or from your own stock.
4. Assemble. If the physical part drifts from the preview, the physical part wins, and you tell the client.
5. Keep the pictures with the job so the next similar build starts faster.

## What people get wrong

They treat the render as a promise of the finished watch. Coatings, printing, and light will differ. They also treat the bench as the place to discover taste, and pay for three bezels to learn they wanted the first one.

Choose the lab for the decision. Choose the bench for the watch.
