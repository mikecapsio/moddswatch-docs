# MODDS.WATCH vs Photo Mockups

A photo mockup pastes parts onto a picture of a watch. An inspiration photo shows someone else's finished mod. Both are flat. MODDS.WATCH puts your combination on a 3D model of that watch and lets you turn it.

## Short answer

Use photos to learn what kinds of mods exist, and use a composite when you only need a quick fake for a slide. Use MODDS.WATCH when the decision is about this case, this face, and this strap together, and you need more than one angle.

## What each one does

### Reference photos

- Fast education. The hobby lives on them.
- They freeze someone else's parts, light, and crop.
- They cannot show your logo on an AE-1200 unless that photo is already your watch.

### Photoshop-style composites

- You control the layers if you have the source images and the skill.
- Perspective, LCD glass, and case thickness are easy to fake badly.
- A second camera angle is a second job.
- The file is an image project, which is useful, and it is not a watch you can spin.

### MODDS.WATCH

- The case is already a 3D model
- Strap, filter, face art, and colors update together
- You orbit to the angle that tells the truth
- Photo Studio then makes the flat picture, on purpose, after the 3D decision
- Only for live models, and only as a visual preview

The interview on the blog once called the product a watch-specific Photoshop. The useful part of that line is "image controls aimed at a watch." The 3D model is the part a normal editor does not give you.

## A workflow that uses all three

1. Save reference photos of mods you like. Do not treat them as the spec.
2. Rebuild the idea on the matching lab. Change whatever does not fit the case.
3. Export Photo Studio frames for the group chat, the client, or the printer.
4. If you still need a poster layout, take those exports into an editor. You are composing approved frames, not inventing the watch.

## Where mockups stay better

- The watch is not in the lab
- You are designing a graphic for a blog, not choosing parts
- You need typography and layout around the watch, which Photo Studio does not do

## Where mockups mislead

- A head-on product photo hides a strap that is too busy from the side
- A pasted dial ignores how the real LCD window crops it
- A heavily graded photo sells a color the parts cannot hit

If the question is "will I like this on the wrist," start from the 3D model, then export the photo. Do not start from the photo and hope the wrist agrees.
