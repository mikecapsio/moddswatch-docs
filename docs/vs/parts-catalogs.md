# MODDS.WATCH vs Parts Catalogs

Parts catalogs sell dials, bezels, hands, crystals, cases, and straps. MODDS.WATCH sells nothing. A catalog photo shows a product the seller can ship. A lab picture shows a combination, including art the seller has never stocked.

Both belong in a build. They answer different questions.

## Short answer

Use a catalog when you know the part number you need. Use MODDS.WATCH when you are still deciding whether that kind of part belongs on the watch, or when the face is your artwork rather than a SKU.

## What each one does

### A parts seller

- Lists a real product with a price
- Shows that product, sometimes on an example watch
- Can tell you, better than any render, whether the part is cut for your reference
- Does not preview your logo, your mixed colors, or your filter stack unless you ask them for a custom job

Shops and kit makers in this hobby, including specialists modders already know, are where the physical pieces come from. The lab does not compete with their inventory.

### MODDS.WATCH

- Previews a look on a supported model
- Accepts your image on the faceplate and on other upload targets
- Links out, through Parts Finder, to generic marketplace listings for tools and common parts
- Does not add your design to a cart
- Does not check that a listing matches the render

Parts Finder is the easy place to confuse the two. Those buttons open Amazon or AliExpress. Some are affiliate links. They are starting points for a spring bar tool, a donor watch, a filter sheet, or a case kit. They are not a generated bill of materials.

## How to use them together

1. Find the direction in the lab. Export pictures.
2. Translate the picture into parts a human can buy: "olive NATO, 22 mm," "negative display filter," "sapphire for an MDV-106," "print this face art."
3. Buy those from a seller you trust. Match the reference number yourself.
4. If no SKU exists, the picture is a brief for a printer or a modder, not a failed search.

## When the catalog is the whole answer

You already know you want a specific insert or a specific dial that sellers photograph clearly, and you have used it before. Opening a 3D lab will not add information.

## When the catalog is the wrong place to start

You have a logo file and a feeling about color. Shopping random faceplates will not show you the logo on the Royale. Upload the logo in the AE-1200 lab, decide if it works, then ask a printer or a modder for the physical face.

The lab will not tell you which of two similar bezels fits. The seller, the part's stated compatibility, or a test fit will.
