# Custom Dials, Logos, and Faceplates

A custom face is one of the highest-impact watch mods, and it is also one of the easiest ways to waste money. Artwork that looks sharp on a laptop can turn to mud at dial size, and a color that seemed right in a screenshot can fight the hands. MODDS.WATCH is the place to catch that before anyone prints.

The long-form hub on the site is https://modds.watch/custom-watch-dial

## Dial versus faceplate

On a mechanical or quartz analog watch, the dial is the face under the hands. A custom dial might be printed, painted, restored, or swapped for an aftermarket part.

On a digital Casio such as the F-91W, A168, or AE-1200, the equivalent is the faceplate: the printed layer you see around and behind the LCD. Modders reskin that layer with new graphics. In the lab, upload the graphic onto the faceplate.

The Duro is closer to a traditional dial. You recolor face elements and can upload art onto parts that accept it, including the bezel insert. You are not designing an LCD faceplate.

## What you can preview

In a lab that has faceplate upload:

1. Make the artwork first, in whatever image tool you already use.
2. Export JPG, PNG, or WebP, at or under 10 MB.
3. Upload it onto the faceplate.
4. Scale and shift it until it sits in the window.
5. Orbit the watch. Check it against the case color, the filter, and the strap.
6. Export Photo Studio pictures of the approved version.

Put the name, initials, photo, or company logo inside that image. The builder applies the picture. It does not typeset a sentence for you. On the AE-1200, the separate Faceplate Text control only recolors lettering that is already part of the model.

Good artwork for a watch face:

- Few elements
- High contrast
- Text large enough to read on a wrist, not on a poster
- A clear margin so type does not collide with the LCD or the hands
- A palette that still works after a color filter, if you plan to use one

## What the preview is for

The exported pictures are a visual reference for:

- You, before you buy a blank faceplate or pay for a print
- A modder who will install it
- A printer who will reproduce the art
- A client or a microbrand approving a logo watch

MODDS.WATCH does not print, cut, or ship the dial. It also does not hand the printer a production template with bleed, safe area, or a factory dieline. Ask the maker what file they need after the look is approved.

## Ways people get a physical custom face

These happen outside the lab. The hub and the blog cover them in more detail.

- UV or pad printing onto a blank dial or faceplate
- Hand or spray painting for a solid color, fumé, or gradient
- Restoration of a damaged original dial, which matters more on a vintage watch than a cheap donor
- An aftermarket dial from a parts seller, which is a catalog part rather than your uploaded image

A logo watch for a company, a gift, or a small brand follows the same path: approve the 3D preview, then pay a modder or a manufacturer to make it. Partnership questions can go to team@modds.watch.

## AE-1200, F-91W, and A168

The AE-1200 has the largest face of the three, so maps, simple illustrations, and wordmarks have room. The F-91W and A168 are small. A full photograph often fails there. Initials, one symbol, or a very simple field of color work better.

Try the stock faceplate treatments before uploading. On the F-91W, the F-91W and F-91WB detail options change the printed layout you are covering.

## Honesty about the result

The 3D face is a preview on an artistic model. A printer's white point, the plastic of a real faceplate, and the tint of a real LCD will not match the screen pixel for pixel. Use the lab to choose the direction and reject weak layouts. Use a physical sample when the exact color is the product.
