# MODDS.WATCH for Hobbyists

MODDS.WATCH is the free way to try a watch mod before you buy a donor watch, a strap, or a tool you might not need. You open a Casio lab in the browser and change the look until you know whether the idea survives contact with the actual case.

It is aimed at people modding for themselves: a first F-91W, a stealth A168, a Royale with a custom face, or a Duro that should look more like a tool watch and less like the stock catalog photo.

## What you get

- Every live lab, with no account and no fee
- A 3D model of that watch, so you see proportions instead of guessing from a product photo
- Room to try directions you would not pay to test: full blackout, a loud strap, a logo you might hate tomorrow
- Photo Studio pictures you can post or keep as the "build this" reference
- Written guides for the words and tools you will meet on the bench

You still buy the watch and the parts somewhere else. You still do the physical work, or you hand the pictures to someone who will.

## A sensible first project

Start with a watch the lab actually has, and with a change you can undo or that costs little.

Good first plans:

- F-91W: strap, a restrained color filter, maybe a simple faceplate
- A168: steel versus NATO, then a stealth or warm-gold direction in Clean Mode
- AE-1200: one faceplate idea and one filter, not five effects at once
- Duro: bezel insert color against the dial and hands, then the strap

The F-91W and A168 are friendly first builds because the watches are inexpensive and the face is simple. The Duro asks more of you if you go past a strap into a crystal or a bezel. Plan that one carefully, then learn the bench skills on purpose.

A Seiko SKX or Seiko 5 is a classic first mechanical mod in the wider hobby. You can read https://modds.watch/blog/best-seiko-watches-to-mod and https://modds.watch/blog/seiko-vs-casio-for-modding. You cannot open those models in the 3D lab yet.

## How to use an evening in the lab

1. Open one lab from https://modds.watch/lab
2. Make three versions, not thirty. Example for an A168: stock, stealth, and one custom face.
3. Orbit each version. If it only works from a flattering angle, it is not done.
4. Export a picture of each.
5. Pick one. Write down the parts that idea implies: donor watch, strap type, filter, or a printed face.
6. Only then open Parts Finder or a seller.

Parts Finder is a set of marketplace links for generic tools and parts. It will not list "your" faceplate, because your upload is not a product they stock.

## Tools, without buying a 150-piece kit

Most first mods need a small set: a spring bar tool for straps, and then, if you open the watch, a case opener, something to press the case back, tweezers, and a loupe. Hand tools and a movement holder matter when you touch dials and hands. A demagnetizer matters on mechanical watches that start running fast, which is a Seiko problem more than an F-91W problem.

The short version is https://modds.watch/tools. The longer kit guide is https://modds.watch/blog/watch-modding-tools-beginner-kit

Buy tools for the mod you actually planned. The lab is how you find out you only needed a strap.

## Budget

Public guidance on the site puts many Casio projects, parts included, in a rough $20–100 range, and many Seiko mods with a better dial, crystal, and bezel at $150–400 or more. Those are ranges, not quotes. A custom print or a hired modder costs extra. Previewing first is how you avoid paying for a second set of parts.

## What to ignore

- Anyone telling you the site will ship the finished watch
- The urge to upload a dense photo onto an F-91W. The face is too small.
- Leaving the tab without a Photo Studio export. There is nothing to log back into.

When the pictures look like a watch you want to wear, move to the bench or to the [modder directory](https://modds.watch/modders).
